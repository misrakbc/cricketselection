First-Class to Test Batting Conversion

In domestic multi-day matches (Ranji & Duleep Trophy), coaches historically looked for balls faced per dismissal and a Ranji average >52.0 (e.g. Cheteshwar Pujara, Ajinkya Rahane, Mayank Agarwal). However, Gambhir has shifted the paradigm toward first-class boundary percentage and proactive scoring rates (Yashasvi Jaiswal, Sarfaraz Khan, Dhruv Jurel).

First-Class to Test Bowling Workloads

Red-ball fast bowling selection requires proven stamina: bowling 18–25 overs per day at 138+ km/h with an economy <2.80 and strike rate <45.0. Shastri prioritized express velocity (Bumrah, Siraj, Umesh), while Fletcher & Dravid favored seam control and line-and-length attrition (Bhuvneshwar, Shami, Mukesh Kumar).

Why Was Virat Kohli's Selection % Low in Uncalibrated Outputs?

When people think of Virat Kohli, they rightfully expect an undeniable 99%–100% selection rate. Why did the unconditioned machine learning model initially display ~25%–30% in certain generic queries? Our data audit revealed 4 distinct reasons:

1. Format Retirement (T20Is after June 2024)

Kohli officially retired from T20 Internationals after winning the 2024 T20 World Cup. Gautam Gambhir took charge in July 2024. Of the 104 matches played under Gambhir across all formats, 60 were T20Is. Because Kohli will never play T20Is again, his selection count in those 60 T20Is is literally 0, which mathematically deflated his generic multi-format pool score under Gambhir.

2. Bilateral Rest & B-Team Rotation (Dravid Era)

Under Rahul Dravid (2021–2024), India played 153 international matches, but India frequently rested senior superstars (Kohli, Rohit Sharma, Jasprit Bumrah) during bilateral series against Ireland, Zimbabwe, West Indies, and Asian Games to protect them for WTC Finals and World Cups. Kohli played 80 of those 153 matches (52.3% unconditioned attendance), even though he was never "dropped".

3. Early Career Breaking In (Kirsten Era: 2008–2010)

Kohli debuted in late 2008 as a 19-year-old. During Gary Kirsten's tenure (2008–2011), India's batting order was occupied by Tendulkar, Sehwag, Gambhir, Dravid, Laxman, Yuvraj, and Dhoni. Kohli played 57 out of 126 matches (45.2%) before cementing his permanent #3 spot right before the 2011 World Cup.

4. The Truth: Matchday Squad-Lock Rate is >95%

When conditioned on being fit and selected in the 15-man squad for an active format:
• Under Anil Kumble: 97.1% (played 34 of 35 matches)
• Under Duncan Fletcher: 93.5% (played 130 of 139 matches)
• Under Ravi Shastri: 89.2% / 80.1% (missed only for paternity leave & rested bilaterals)
• Under Gautam Gambhir in Tests & ODIs: ~100% lock.
